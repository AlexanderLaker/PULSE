# The PRISM Analysis on the Drivers page

Concept and rules for the per-driver reading that the Drivers page shows under "PRISM Analysis", rebuilt for the 51-driver base on the owner's instruction of 17 September 2026. This document is the specification; `scripts/build_driver_analysis.py` produces the figures, `data/trend_base_2026-09/driver_analysis_v1.json` carries the readings, and `tests/test_driver_analysis.py` holds both to the rules below.

## 1. What the panel is for

Everything an expert scores is already on the page: the direction, the probability, the GP1 share, the peak year and curve, the uncertainty score, the sources, the category, regional and value-chain exposure. None of that tells the reader what the driver does to the number on the dashboard. A driver with the largest Shift value in the base can be a small item in the portfolio because it sits in a region that holds a twentieth of the pool; a driver with modest scores can be the largest single headwind because it reaches every cell. The exposure grid says where a mechanism applies; only the roll-up says where the gross profit is.

The PRISM Analysis therefore answers one question per driver, in four parts:

1. How much of the dashboard number this driver carries, and why that much.
2. Where that effect lands once the estimated gross-profit mix is applied.
3. When it arrives inside the 2026 to 2035 horizon.
4. What the model's band for it hinges on, which drivers move with it, and what would change the read.

It is an analysis of the model's own arithmetic. It does not restate scores, it does not forecast, and it does not recommend. Under the ceteris-paribus design (D16) every contribution reads as exposure if nobody acts, and the strategic response belongs to the reader.

## 2. The figures behind it

The engine compounds, per category x region cell and year, one factor per force: `1 + force_weight x attenuation x dampening x sum over the force's drivers of (probability draw x GP1 share x direction x materialisation x category exposure / 5 x regional exposure / 5)`. Every driver's term inside that sum is linear in its own probability draw, so the mean of the Monte Carlo term is the term evaluated at the score mean (probability / 6, the Beta-prior mean of F5) with the materialisation averaged over the driver's own peak-jitter grid (O7). `scripts/build_driver_analysis.py` evaluates exactly this, driver by driver and cell by cell, spreads the compounding cross-terms (which the cell shift at the means already contains) across the drivers of each cell in proportion to their additive terms so that the drivers of a cell add up to the cell shift, and rolls the cells up with the same gross-profit shares the run used (O6). The sum of the 51 contributions is then the run's terminal-year mean, to a few hundredths of a percent (5.546 points at the means against a run mean of 5.545 on the 2.12.0 base; the only residual is the covariance between the force factors, which the Monte Carlo averages and an evaluation at the means does not). The dashboard shows the median, and the portfolio distribution is skewed, so the median sits 0.03 points below the mean. The decomposition is therefore scaled by one factor, the run's median over the sum at the means, 1.006 on the 2.12.0 base, so that the contributions add up to the headline the reader sees. The factor is the mean-to-median skew of the portfolio distribution, not an approximation error, and it is recorded with the reference run.

This is an expected-value decomposition of the model, not a fitted attribution and not a second simulation. It is labelled attribution wherever it is shown (D6), and the reading says so in its closing line. The record `data/trend_base_2026-09/driver_attribution_v1.json` carries per driver:

- the terminal-year contribution to the portfolio shift in points, by year, its rank among the 51 and among the headwinds or tailwinds, its share of the gross headwind (7.3 points) or gross tailwind (1.7 points) and its share of the same-signed drivers of its force;
- the contribution by category x region cell, by category and by region, with each cell's share of the pool, the driver's share of that cell's shift and the same-force drivers it shares the cell with;
- the expected materialisation by year and the share of the terminal-year effect present in each year (the additive term factorises, so this is the materialisation ratio and never exceeds one);
- the driver's own P10 to P90 range at 2035 from its Beta prior and its peak jitter, and its share of the portfolio band under the configured copula (the Euler allocation `Cov(term, sum) / Var(sum)` over 200,000 draws of the engine's own samplers; the shares add up to one, and a tailwind's share can be negative because its draws move with the headwinds of its force);
- the structural chain that turns the row's Shift value into a portfolio contribution: the pass-through of its force (force weight x attenuation, 6.3 to 8.1 percent), its reach, and the effective overlap correction it faces. Reach is the share of the gross-profit mix the driver's exposures cover: the sum over the 52 cells of the cell's share times category exposure / 5 times regional exposure / 5. A driver scored 5 in every cell reaches 100 percent of the pool, one scored 1 everywhere 4 percent; the same exposure profile reaches more of the pool when it sits on the large cells. The record also names, over all 52 cells, the cell in which the driver is the largest share of the cell's own shift, so that a reading can say "its largest share of any cell" and mean it.

The reference run is the production recipe (3 x 50,000 iterations, master seed 42) on the committed seed and the default configuration, and it is stamped with a digest of everything it depends on (the scoring state of every driver, the onset years, the force layers, the cell weights, the path, the engine version): a build on a seed or configuration whose digest differs is refused, and `--check` fails, so a re-scored seed cannot carry readings reconciled to a median the current model does not produce. The record carries the persisted production run it reproduces (`persisted_run`: run 99 of 13 September 2026, read from that run's QA workbook with `--workbook FILE`, with its 13 terminal-year category quantiles), and `--check` fails if the reference run no longer reproduces those quantiles exactly, so the readings can never quietly describe a run other than the one on the dashboard (portfolio 2035 median -5.58 percent, P10 -6.16, P90 -4.89). When the finance cell weights arrive (O6), the record and the readings are regenerated with `--cell-weights FILE`, exactly as the production run is, and the new run's workbook is recorded with it.

## 3. The four parts, and what each may say

Every reading has four labelled paragraphs and a closing basis line. The labels are fixed; the Drivers page renders them as small headings.

**Weight in the answer.** The contribution in points against the 5.6-point net exposure (or the 1.7-point gross tailwind for an expansion driver), its rank, and the reason for its size expressed through the chain: Shift value, pass-through, reach, overlap. The reason is the substance: a reader must understand why a driver with a large Shift value carries little, or why a mid-scored driver carries much. Comparisons within the force are encouraged when they explain something ("72 percent of the Competitive force's headwind").

**Where it lands.** The cells that carry the effect, with their points, the regional split of the effect against the exposure profile, and the categories where the driver is a large share of the category's own shift. This is where the mix matters: the same exposure score produces a fifth of the effect in a region that holds a twentieth of the pool. Where two or three drivers of the same force sit on the same cell, the reading names them, because the overlap correction already discounts them together and a reader must not add them as if they were independent.

**When it lands.** The share of the terminal-year effect in the numbers by 2028 and by 2030, the half-way year, and what the curve does inside the horizon: a front-loaded shock that is essentially complete by 2028, a staircase of dated gates, a demographic drift that keeps growing to the horizon, a late instrument whose peak can slip past 2035. Dated instruments are named with their dates, because the timing of a regulatory driver is not a judgment, it is in law.

**What the band hinges on.** The driver's own P10 to P90 range at 2035 in points, its share of the portfolio band's variance when it is material, the single condition the width depends on (an oil path, a fee level not yet set, a share transfer that may not happen, a sign that could flip), the drivers that move with it, and the observable that would change the read. The observable must be something the sources in the driver already measure or a dated decision: a panel series, a regulatory act, a company print. This is the paragraph a reader uses to decide whether to re-score. One thing the paragraph is honest about by construction: the width of a driver's own range is set by its probability and uncertainty scores alone (the Beta prior and the peak-year jitter), so the same two scores always give the same width relative to the expected value. The "because" in this paragraph is therefore the rationale behind the uncertainty score, the thing the width stands for, not a property the model computed; a reading may call a range narrow or wide for its size only against that fixed scale.

**Basis line.** One sentence, appended by the renderer from the record, naming the base, the cell-weight basis and the headline the figures reconcile to, in terms the reader can check against the dashboard: "Figures: model attribution on the 2.12.0 base, estimated HCB gross-profit mix, reconciled to a 2035 portfolio median of -5.6 percent (3 x 50,000 iterations, seed 42). Read as exposure if nobody acts, not as a forecast." A dashboard that shows a different headline is the signal that the readings have aged.

## 4. What a reading must never contain

- A restated score. No "probability 5 of 5", no "GP1 22 percent", no exposure numbers, no uncertainty score. The page shows them; the reading interprets what they produce.
- Any reference to the review that produced the base, to earlier bases, to what was corrected, deleted, merged or renamed, or to the age of a source. The reading describes the driver as it stands.
- A management response. No "Henkel should", no lever, no mitigation. D16 holds strategy constant; the response belongs to the reader.
- A fact that is not in the driver's own description or sources. The reading adds arithmetic and interpretation, not new evidence. No test can hold this rule; it is held by the author and by the review of every regeneration, and the adversarial review of 17 September 2026 checked it driver by driver.
- A model figure typed by hand. Every number the model produces is a placeholder filled from the record, so that a re-scored driver, a recalibration or a new cell-weight file cannot leave a stale figure in a text. Facts of the driver itself (dates of instruments, the figures its sources report) are typed, because they are inputs, not outputs; a re-scoring trigger the author sets ("a scalp share of salon retail above 5 percent would argue for the upper end of the range") is judgement and is worded as such. The peak year is a score too and is named only through `{peak_year}` (the onset year through `{onset_year}`, the curve's name through `{curve}`). Two locks read the templates, where a model figure can only be a placeholder in braces: one fails on the shapes a model figure takes in the prose (a share of the pool, of the effect, of a cell or of the headline; a contribution in points; a materialisation year; a pass-through; a reach; a rank; the driver's peak year after "to", "at" or "until"), the other renders every driver's fields and fails if any rendered value appears in the template as a bare number followed by "percent of the", "points", a region or a materialisation year. Word fractions that describe the mix ("a twentieth of the pool") are the author's arithmetic on the record and are reviewed, not locked; where the record offers the figure, the placeholder is used instead (`{pool_lhc}`, `{pool_hair}`, the regional shares).
- Typographic and rhetorical markers: no em-dashes, no bullet points inside the text, no two-sentence aphorisms, no exclamation. The register is that of a written report.

## 5. How the numbers are worded

Contributions are stated in points of the relative GP1 shift with two decimals ("0.90 of the 5.6 points"), so that drivers a few hundredths apart can be told apart and the stated ranks checked; the portfolio totals (net 5.6, gross headwind 7.3, gross tailwind 1.7) keep the headline's one decimal. Shares are whole percentages. Ranks are ordinals among the 51 and among the 33 headwinds or 18 tailwinds; two drivers can sit a ten-thousandth of a point apart (C-24 and C-10 at 2.12.0), so a regeneration reviews the ordinals where the record shows a near tie. Cells and categories are named with the labels the page shows, in lower case ("fabric clean Europe", "hard-surface cleaners"), so a reader can find the cell in the Shift Matrix without a second table; the driver's own Shift column is called its "Shift value". Drivers are cited by code and, on first mention, a short name in the author's words (never a restated title). "Points" always means percentage points of the portfolio or category shift; "percent of the pool" always means a share of the estimated gross-profit mix.

Two denominators appear side by side and are always named: a headwind's contribution is stated against the 5.6 points of net exposure (what the dashboard shows), its share of its force against the force's gross headwind (what the force's contraction drivers add up to), and a tailwind against the 7.3 points of gross headwind it offsets and the 1.7-point gross tailwind. The 33 headwind contributions add up to 7.3 points, not 5.6; the 18 tailwinds offset 1.7 of them.

Each contribution is the mean of the driver's term, scaled by the factor of section 2 so that the 51 add up to the median headline; the driver's own P50 sits slightly off its mean because the Beta prior is skewed, which is why the reading quotes the contribution and the P10 to P90 as the range, and never a P50.

## 6. Provenance shown on the page

The Drivers page renders the four paragraphs with their labels, the basis line in small type, and under the card one fixed footnote: "Attribution at the score means, reconciled to the persisted run's headline; a reading authored on those figures. Read as exposure if nobody acts, not as a forecast." The card is read-only for every role. The former note that the analysis "is generated by the engine" is replaced, because the engine does not write prose: the figures are generated, the reading is authored on them.

The category drill-down (`CategoryDetailPanel`) ranks the contributing drivers with the same arithmetic (`lib/driverAttribution.ts`, a port of the decomposition pinned to the Python record by a parity fixture), so the ranking a reader sees in the drill-down and the weight a reading states for the same driver agree. The drill-down rescales each row's drivers to that row's own terminal-year median where the readings are reconciled to the portfolio median with one factor, so shares agree exactly and points can differ by the row's own factor (a few tenths of a percent on the 2.12.0 base); its footer says so, names the basis, and counts any drivers taken at full materialisation because they carry no peak year or curve. The basis is one per row: the decomposition for every driver when the configuration layers and the run's cell weights are served, the older `Shift x category exposure` proxy for every driver otherwise, labelled as such, never a mix. Before this change the drill-down used the proxy alone, which ignored regional exposure, the cell weights, attenuation and the overlap correction and placed a growth-market driver among the largest laundry headwinds in Europe (F-30).

The Description card above the PRISM Analysis is the driver's description from the base file. It carried the review's own notes ("not the 45 percent the old base assumed", "could not be verified", "the unverified figure is dropped") in 40 of the 51 drivers, next to a card rebuilt to carry none; since the same instruction the base file is `core_set_51_v6.json`, v5 with those notes taken out of the descriptions and nothing else changed, and the review's record stays in the `change` field, which nothing renders. `scripts/apply_driver_analysis.py` moves a database's descriptions with the readings.

## 7. Regeneration and the staleness lock

The readings depend on the seed, the calibration, the cell weights and the reference run. `python3 scripts/build_driver_analysis.py --check` recomputes the record and re-renders every text from its template and fails when either differs from the committed file; CI runs it next to `generate_seed_from_core_set.py --check` and `build_estimated_cell_weights.py --check`. `scripts/generate_seed_from_core_set.py` reads the rendered text into `strategic_implication`, so the seed and the readings cannot drift apart either.

The database copy takes precedence over the seed (the `trends` table). `scripts/apply_driver_analysis.py` moves it: archive-first, compare-and-swap on the current text of every driver, one transaction, `--postgres` required for Neon, a dry run that reports and never writes. It writes the reading into `strategic_implication` and moves a description that still reads as v5 onto v6 (a description an admin has reworded is reported and left alone). It refuses a database that lacks a driver of the base (exit 8) and one whose scoring inputs differ from the seed's (exit 9): a reading is written on the seed's scores, so after an admin re-score in the tool the re-score is carried into the base file and the readings regenerated before the script runs (`--allow-drift` writes anyway, with the drift listed). It is run on the local SQLite copy and, right before the production deploy, on Neon, followed by a redeploy (the API serves the driver list from a per-instance cache and an admin edit writes every column of the cached driver back) and by the request that admins reload any open Drivers page before they edit (the editor sends the description it loaded on every save); a second `--dry-run` after the deploy must report nothing to change and nothing to move. No score, exposure, source, provenance flag or expert proposal is touched, so the next production run reports no input drift.

Operator sequence when the inputs change (a re-scored driver, a recalibration, a finance cell-weight file, a new production run): `run_50k_prod.py` as today, then `build_driver_analysis.py --reference --workbook <the run's QA workbook>` (with the same `--cell-weights FILE` if one was used), review the re-rendered texts and their comparative claims, regenerate the seed (`generate_seed_from_core_set.py`), run `apply_driver_analysis.py` (after `apply_driver_vocabulary.py` on a database not yet moved by O15), deploy, redeploy after the Neon run.

## 8. Known limits, stated rather than hidden

- The decomposition attributes the compounding cross-terms proportionally among the drivers of a cell; the cell totals themselves are exact at the means. The sum reproduces the run's mean to a few hundredths of a percent; the one scale factor is the mean-to-median skew of the portfolio distribution (section 2), so each contribution is a scaled mean and the total a median. The additive-sum band of the band analysis is within 0.06 points of the run's band at both ends.
- Comparative claims in the prose ("the widest of any driver", "one of the broadest reaches in the base") are the author's reading of the record on the day of the basis line. The figures inside them regenerate; the comparatives do not, which is why the operator sequence above ends with a review of the re-rendered texts, why the texts prefer comparatives with a wide margin, and why every comparative was verified against the record in the adversarial review of 17 September 2026 (the table is in `docs/governance/DECISION_LOG.md` Part L).
- The band share is an Euler allocation over the additive sum, computed with the engine's own copula and jitter samplers. It is exact for the additive sum, not for the compounded total, and it is quoted only for headwinds whose share is material.
- The readings are between 200 and 600 words whatever the driver's weight, because the question "why is it small" needs as much explanation as "why is it large"; length is not proportional to weight by design.
- The readings are written on the estimated HCB gross-profit mix (O14), an estimate from public reporting, not Henkel P&L. Where a reading says a cell holds a share of the pool, that is the estimate's share. The finance file replaces it, and the readings are regenerated with it.
- A reading is a snapshot of the model on the day of its basis line. An admin edit to a score after that day changes the drill-down at once and the reading only at the next regeneration; the basis line is what tells the reader which day the reading describes.
